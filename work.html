Our work

What we actually do

Four programmes, one aim: institutions that plan for disabled people before they arrive.

Programme one

Awareness sessions

Interactive sessions in schools, colleges and universities that change how students and staff think about disability.

We have run sessions in institutions including Tagore International School, working with students, teachers and administrators together rather than separately — because the gap between what a school intends and what a student experiences usually sits between those groups.

Sessions cover the social model of disability, everyday accessibility, inclusive language, and what to do when you get it wrong. They are co-facilitated with disabled speakers.

Book a session

Programme two

Expert-led webinars

Open online sessions with disability advocates, educators, technologists and researchers.

Webinars let us reach people far outside the cities we can travel to, and they let disabled experts speak without having to navigate an inaccessible venue to do it. Recordings and write-ups are published on our blog.

Browse past webinars on the Enable Education blog (opens in a new tab)

Programme three

In-person dialogues

Face-to-face conversations where disabled people set the agenda and institutions come to listen.

A dialogue is not a panel with a token speaker. Disabled participants decide the questions; educators, employers and officials answer them. We host these in accessible venues, with interpretation and materials arranged in advance rather than on request.

Read dialogue reports on the Enable Education blog (opens in a new tab)

Programme four

Funlish workshops

English-language sessions designed so that children of different abilities learn in the same room, at the same time.

Funlish uses games, movement and storytelling instead of drills. Every activity has more than one way in — spoken, written, drawn or acted — so no child is sitting out while the others take part.

Bring Funlish to your school

In short

We do four things.

We visit schools and colleges to talk about disability. We run online talks with experts. We hold meetings where disabled people ask the questions. And we run fun English classes that every child can join.
